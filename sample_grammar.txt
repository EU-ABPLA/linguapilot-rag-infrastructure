English Verb Tenses: A Beginner's Guide

The Past Simple
The past simple is used to describe actions that happened and were completed in the past. Regular verbs add -ed to form the past simple: walk → walked, play → played. Irregular verbs have unique past forms: go → went, see → saw, eat → ate. Negative: I did not go. Question: Did you see it?

The Present Simple
The present simple describes habitual actions, general truths, or permanent states. Use the base verb for I/you/we/they and add -s/-es for he/she/it. Examples: I work, she works, they study. Negative: I do not work. Question: Do you work here?

The Future Simple
The future simple is formed using "will" plus the base verb. It expresses predictions or future actions without planning. Example: I will go tomorrow, she will call you. Negative: I will not go. Question: Will you come?

Continuous Tenses
The continuous (progressive) form uses "be" plus the -ing form of the verb. Present continuous: I am working. Past continuous: I was working. Future continuous: I will be working. These describe actions in progress at a specific moment.

Modal Verbs
Modal verbs like can, could, may, might, must, should, would express ability, permission, obligation, or possibility. Example: You should study harder. I can speak English. These do not change form for different subjects.
